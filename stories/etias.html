Travel and visas

ETIAS explained: who needs it, the €20 fee, and why there is still no start date

Everything about the EU’s travel authorisation for visa-exempt visitors: eligibility, cost, validity, how to apply, processing times, refusals and appeals.

Verified 20 Nov 2026

ETIAS, the European Travel Information and Authorisation System, is an online pre-travel check for people who can visit Europe without a visa. It is not a visa. It is not operating yet: the EU has not set a launch date, and most observers now expect 2027. The EU has promised to announce the date several months in advance. Until then there is nothing to apply for.

Who needs itNationals of 59 visa-exempt countries and territories (for example the UK, US, Canada, Australia, Japan, the UAE)
Where it applies30 European countries: the 29 Schengen countries plus Cyprus
Fee€20. Free for applicants under 18 or over 70, and for family members of EU, Icelandic, Liechtenstein, Norwegian or Swiss nationals
Validity3 years, or until the passport expires, whichever comes first; multiple trips
Stay allowedUp to 90 days in any 180-day period. Days in Cyprus are counted separately
Decision timeUsually minutes; at the latest 96 hours. Up to 14 more days if extra documents are requested, or 30 days if you are invited to an interview
StatusNot launched; no start date

Who does not need ETIAS

Anyone who needs a Schengen visa (they apply for the visa instead, not ETIAS); EU citizens; and people holding a residence permit or long-stay visa from one of the 30 countries. Children do need their own ETIAS, though without a fee; a parent or guardian applies for them.

What you will need to apply

A passport issued within the last 10 years and valid for at least three months after the day you plan to leave Europe; an email address; a payment card; and answers about your personal details, education and job, your first planned country of stay, and background questions (serious criminal convictions in the past 20 years, travel to war or conflict zones, and previous removal orders). Students give the name of their school or university.

How to apply

Only through the official ETIAS website or the official EU app, once launched. Use the same passport you will travel with: if you renew your passport, you need a new ETIAS. Apply before you buy tickets or book hotels. You can renew in the last 120 days before expiry.

If you are refused

The refusal email gives the reason, names the country that took the decision, and explains how to appeal and the deadline. Appeals are handled under that country’s national law. Common grounds for refusal: a passport reported lost or stolen, an entry ban in the Schengen Information System, a security or health risk assessment, or failing to answer a request for information or attend an interview. You can still apply for a Schengen visa.

The transition period

After launch there will be a transitional period of about six months in which ETIAS is optional, then a grace period: a traveller entering for the first time after it ends may be let in exceptionally once. After that, airlines and border guards will refuse travellers without one.

What applies at the border now: EES

The Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully operational since 10 April 2026 in 29 countries. It replaces passport stamps and records your fingerprints and a facial image on your first crossing. Allow extra time.

Beware of copycat sites. Websites that offer to “pre-register” you or charge more than €20 are not official. If you need a visa instead, the standard Schengen short-stay fee for adults is €90.
